# Sigma Contribution Provenance Is Underrepresented

## Observed Signal

- Sigma observed that material human contributions were often visible in conversation and screenshots but only the later AI-condensed conclusion appeared as a durable lineage artifact.

## Source

- Source: Sigma feedback and supplied screenshots during the current Business-development stabilization conversation.
- Recorder: Anchor preserved the signal as a durable Feedback artifact after Sigma identified the provenance gap.

## Interpretation

- The current artifactization pattern can preserve correct conclusions while underrepresenting the human observation and judgment that caused those conclusions to exist.
- If left unchanged, the same bias would weaken role training, contribution review, and future Process derivation from real artifact behavior.

## Feedback Target

- Target: current Tiinex Business-development artifactization and cross-role lineage practice
- Related Surface: [Tiinex Core](../initiatives/001-core-project.trace.md)
- Related Role: [Sigma](../roles/001-sigma-role.trace.md)

## Feedback Received

- Source: Sigma
- Observation: materially important Sigma input is often condensed into a later Anchor, Axiom, or Loom artifact without preserving enough first-class provenance to show what the human actually observed, questioned, prioritized, or contributed.
- Consequence: the resulting lineage can remain semantically correct while appearing disproportionately AI-authored, which makes Sigma's role harder to understand, teach, reproduce, or evaluate.
- Process Impact: future Processes learned from real artifacts would be biased if human observations are routinely absorbed into AI summaries rather than represented as their own feedback/evidence signals.
- Evidence:
  - [Business tree observation](assets/007-1-business-tree-observation.png) — Sigma used the visible artifact topology to identify continuity and contribution-shape concerns.
  - [Lineage continuity observation](assets/007-2-lineage-continuity-observation.png) — Sigma used the visible lineage surface to identify structural issues that were not originally raised by the active AI role.
- Additional examples from this tranche include Sigma raising the `.cache` concern, the Parent/leaf continuity issue, access levels 0/1/2, bounded semi-automation, browser-companion ergonomics, safety-check latency observations, and the need for an inheritable Practitioner improvement baseline.

## Disposition

- State: accepted
- Follow-Up: [Role Contribution And Human Evidence Provenance](../initiatives/001-5-role-contribution-and-human-evidence-provenance-task.trace.md)
- Practice Change: when Sigma or another participant materially changes scope, priority, acceptance, design, risk understanding, or observed-system understanding, preserve that contribution as first-class Feedback, Evidence, Decision input, or another appropriate artifact before downstream condensation erases its role-level provenance.
- Noise Boundary: ordinary conversational turns do not require artifacts merely because they occurred; the preservation threshold is material contribution to interpretation, direction, acceptance, evidence, or repeatable working behavior.

## Limits

- This artifact summarizes the received feedback; it is not a verbatim transcript of the host conversation.
- The screenshots are supporting observation material and do not make the current Viewer or host UI authoritative for Tiinex semantics.
- This feedback does not imply that AI-authored synthesis is undesirable; it requires the synthesis to preserve material source/contributor provenance rather than silently replacing it.
- This artifact does not define the final Process schema or a universal attribution taxonomy.
