# Sigma Foundation Workflow Feedback

## Observed Signal

- Sigma reports that Foundation transport and Tooling ergonomics are now materially affecting human workflow trust: full-source continuity and shared multi-role cold starts are working, while repeated broad validation can amplify expensive external interruptions.

## Source

- Source: direct Sigma workflow observation during the current Foundation collaboration, including two independent cold-started Axiom/Loom sessions and repeated host safety-check interruptions.

## Interpretation

- Interpretation: the transport direction is gaining trust, while validation/checkpoint efficiency is not merely a local runtime optimization; redundant Tiinex-owned work can compound external waiting costs and should therefore be reduced without weakening closure or host safeguards.

## Feedback Target

- Target: Tiinex Foundation inter-role Handoff transport, Tooling validation/checkpoint workflow, and later Viewer workflow comparison.

## Feedback Received

- Source: Sigma.
- Full-source continuity: during Foundation, inter-role Handoff carriers should preserve complete Business, Docs, and Site source turn-to-turn even when semantic task scope is narrow. A lost carried Workspace must not be silently reconstructed from GitHub and treated as equivalent continuity.
- Carrier convenience: when no earlier carrier major is known, an exported carrier may begin at major 001; carrier lineage is primarily a consumer-facing progress/retry/stability projection rather than global history authority.
- Multi-role transport observation: one shared full-source carrier with separate route text cold-started both Axiom and Loom successfully without pre-context, and both roles returned correctly qualified transports.
- Validation friction observation: additional host safety checks sometimes appear as false-positive workflow interruptions and have been observed to add roughly 15–25 minutes per occurrence. Repeated or redundant broad validation therefore has meaningful human workflow cost beyond local process runtime and can reduce confidence in Tiinex workflow predictability.
- Safety boundary: the desired response is not to bypass or evade host safeguards. Tiinex should avoid unnecessary repeated work by making focused validation, checkpoint reuse, restartability, and final closure boundaries explicit.
- Viewer observation: the refactored Viewer still has ergonomic/user-friendliness work remaining. Sigma plans side-by-side PoC/refactor testing after the current correctness/Foundation contracts are stable; create/update/export/publish behavior has not yet been accepted through that comparison.
- Worked-time observation: the latest cold-start Axiom and Loom turns reported approximately 11m50s and 13m44s of worked time respectively. Sigma treats these as lower-bound execution signals because host safety checks, UI lag, model wait, and other external overhead are not included.
- Return-transport observation: Axiom's return preserved the expected canonical full-source carrier shape, while one Loom return omitted Business/Docs source and used a visibly different carrier convention. Sigma flagged this as evidence that canonical return manufacture may have been bypassed or regressed even though the work result itself remained useful.
- Living-role representation: Sigma should remain visible through explicit observation, decision, constraint, acceptance/rejection, and follow-up evidence when those materially affect Tiinex; co-authorship alone is not sufficient durable representation of the human role.
- Process/role improvement expectation: when repeated work reveals a missing subprocess, role blind spot, convention drift, or recurring human teaching burden, Anchor should route a durable process/role/tooling improvement rather than leave the real operating method in chat memory.
- Commit ergonomics request: Sigma wants a deterministic non-LLM commit-message workflow derived from staged Tiinex artifacts, preferably rendering one line per affected artifact as `.topics/<path>/<dimension> [<type>] <Why>`, exposed through an easy VS Code task in Business, Docs, and Site. `Why` is preferred because it records rationale more reliably than title/summary and reduces repeated AI/token cost.

## Disposition

- State: accepted
- Follow-Up: Anchor preserves this feedback in Foundation routing; Loom validation-efficiency work should treat redundant validation/checkpoint reuse as a high-priority workflow issue while keeping final closure intact; Viewer UX remains deferred until Sigma performs the planned comparison.

## Limits

- Fidelity: condensed durable summary of Sigma statements from the active collaboration; timings are human-observed workflow estimates rather than instrumented Tiinex benchmark measurements.
- Boundary: this feedback does not prove why a host safety check occurred, does not claim Tiinex controls host moderation, does not authorize bypass behavior, and does not constitute final Viewer or Foundation acceptance.
