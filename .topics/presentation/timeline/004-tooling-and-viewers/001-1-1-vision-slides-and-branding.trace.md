# When the vision was easier to generate than to explain

In **May 2026**, Tiinex had already begun experimenting with public-facing visual material. AI-generated slides attempted to communicate a collection of ideas that was still changing faster than any concise explanation of them.

The first images and an early Tiinex identity file appear together in a **13 May** repository revision. On **14 May**, the slides were revised under `assets/slides/v1`. Their subjects included quiet drift, collaboration, transparency, connections to reality and keeping observations visible. These were attempts to *explain* the ideas, not verification that the corresponding functionality was already implemented.

## Original material, at its historical revision

- [The V1 image deck, as revised 14 May](https://github.com/Tiinex/.github/tree/0786799b2078fd6acd7d238a1af671c10244c0dd/assets/slides/v1) — archived presentation experiment, not a product maturity demonstration.
- [Early Tiinex identity image, at the 13 May revision](https://github.com/Tiinex/.github/blob/2dd092c950b42b906179af8b2097fd788b746cb7/assets/tiinex.png) — a dated snapshot, not today's identity.
- [V2 feedback-package document, 14 May](https://github.com/Tiinex/.github/blob/eae2f9aee2bdbbae5fcd12aea0adfc9be1e96f50/assets/slides/v2/feedback-package.md) — retained evaluation of the communication experiment.

## What the evaluation recorded

The V2 document names information density, an unclear intended audience, repeating AI-generated visual patterns, inconsistent logos and insufficient observable demonstration. It argues that a deck cannot carry the whole system by itself. These are **statements retained in a historical feedback document**; without a separately verified attribution, do not present its comments as independent audience consensus or claim that a slide experiment was a measured usability trial.

## How this belongs in the story

The work illustrates a persistent distinction: **a visually coherent promise is not the same thing as an inspectable implementation**. Later artifact/lineage viewers offer a way for someone to follow the underlying sources instead of relying only on slides. This is a retrospective editorial connection, not a proven assertion that the V1 deck *caused* the Viewer to be built.

Visual identity kept evolving. The May image and the later September Tiinex identity image are distinct versions (see the system-map chapter), but the complete intermediate design iteration chain is not reconstructed here.
