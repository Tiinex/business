# The continuity protocol was a screen recording

For a time, when the next ChatGPT conversation needed context, the developer recorded the screen and sent the video to another session. The recipient could inspect what had been discussed and navigate the captured material. This was a pragmatic **manual continuity transfer**, not a formal Handoff Package.

The developer reported many recordings, including a batch dated July 23–24, 2026. At the time, a full ChatGPT account export could take days to arrive and a share link was not an attachable reference within another conversation in the way this workflow needed. The video workaround was practical but bulky, difficult to inspect, difficult to compare and hard to automate responsibly.

## What the workaround could and could not carry

A video might preserve visible decisions and browsing context. It did **not** intrinsically provide a machine-qualified artifact graph, immutable parent identity, explicit authority or exact task frontier. It also created a significant burden on the receiving model: reconstruct the project from observations of an interface.

## Evidence status

- **Firsthand developer account and screenshot of dated recordings:** supplied during the October 2026 historical reconstruction.
- **Not published as evidence here:** video files, private account exports and their contents.
- **Do not infer:** that the first continuity schema or earliest handoff-like experiment began only after July; some preceded these recordings.

## The design pressure

The developer needed a transfer that was smaller, more explicit, more sustainable and inspectable. That pressure explains the later focus on tooling, portable snapshots, and recipient grounding more accurately than a retrospective story about wanting to create an abstract provenance standard.
