# Reader guide: inspect Tiinex without a live presenter

**If you have never heard of Tiinex, start here.** This is independent of any single live demonstration, standards group or meeting.

## The problem

A long AI-assisted project may contain useful work distributed across conversations, files, tools and people. When a session ends, starts over or changes hosts, simply pasting a summary can silently lose important context: *what work is current, which sources support a decision, what is unverified, and who is actually allowed to do the next step?*

## The five things to keep separate

1. **Artifact:** a durable readable record (often Markdown) with a declared schema and inspectable content.
2. **Lineage:** explicit links between artifacts; a Parent explains declared continuation, not automatic real-world causation.
3. **Evidence:** source material that may support a claim but does not become conclusive simply because it is cited.
4. **Qualification:** checking declared structure, integrity and interpretive boundaries using appropriate tooling.
5. **Handoff / carrier:** a scoped transfer of work or responsibility, and the bytes/package that convey the qualified source. A carrier is not the Handoff's authority by itself.

## A small walk-through

Imagine a reviewer receives a ZIP. A healthy review asks: *What is the declared entrypoint? Which Workspaces are included? Is the current role entitled to take the requested action? What evidence and open questions accompany it? Does the output preserve its actual Parent and target?* The recipient should not infer these things merely from the archive's filenames.

Use chapter 003 for early durable Topics; chapter 006 for the screen-recording problem; chapters 007–009 for the explicit system boundaries and package route.

## Where to inspect the evidence

**Read the sources beside the claim they support.** The first-hand history is in epoch 001. The early YouTube proof experiment is documented in epoch 002. Portable Markdown source records and the envelope design appear in epoch 003. Viewer and feedback-ingress implementations belong to epoch 004. The deliberate ChatGPT Web monolith test is in epoch 005. Epochs 006–009 cover video, Courier, the refactor, Playthings and the distinct Handoff/Package contracts.

This guide deliberately exposes **no historical source attachments of its own**. In the PoC viewer, open the individual Topic whose claim you want to test; its Referenced Material should contain only sources directly relevant to that specific Topic.

## Known limits and an invitation

The historical chapters are not a product maturity claim. Some origins are grounded in firsthand recollection; GitHub provides evidence that files existed at least by specific commits. Tests can show bounded behavior under controlled conditions, but cannot prove universal correctness. Critical feedback is most useful when it names an exact claim or artifact version, a concrete observation, any contradictory evidence and uncertainty. The separate `work/w3c` Task is an example of a recipient-specific review request; it is **not** part of this general history.
